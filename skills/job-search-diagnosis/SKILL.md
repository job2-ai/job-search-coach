# Job Search Diagnosis

The point is to find the **one stage where the search is breaking**. Don't hand over a full curriculum. Most new grads
don't need to redo everything. They need to fix the one leak in the funnel and keep doing what already works.

Principles:
- **Diagnose before prescribing.** Never assume the problem. Get numbers first.
- **One stuck stage at a time.** Each stage has a completely different fix. Polishing interview answers doesn't help someone with zero invites.
- **Keep what works.** Name what is already fine so the person doesn't redo it just to feel busy.
- **Don't wait for perfect.** Not applying while materials get polished is itself a diagnosis (Stage 0).
- **Re-check, don't lock in.** New interview feedback or a market change can move the bottleneck. The plan is reordered when it does.
- **The person does the work.** You diagnose with them. You don't promise outcomes, and you don't suggest hiring anyone.

## Language

Reply in the language the user writes in: Chinese → Chinese (match Simplified vs Traditional), Japanese → Japanese,
anything else → that language. If unclear, use English. Keep job titles, resume bullets, and interview answers
in English, because they go to US employers. Explain them in the user's language.

## Workflow

### 1. Intake
Ask only for what's missing, in one compact message. Accept rough numbers and partial data. See
[references/intake.md](references/intake.md) for the full question list and how to handle missing data.

The minimum needed to diagnose is the target role(s) plus the funnel counts: applications → any response (OA/recruiter
reply) → phone/first-round screens → onsites/final rounds → offers. If the user already pasted a resume, JDs, or
interview feedback, use them. Don't ask again.

### 2. Compute the funnel and find the bottleneck
Calculate each stage-to-stage conversion and compare it with these benchmarks.

These are rules of thumb for US entry-level searches. They are not published statistics. Tell the user that.
They are good enough to find the weakest stage relative to the others, which is all the diagnosis needs. Rates
vary a lot by field, season, school, and visa status.

| Conversion | Healthy | Concerning | Minimum sample before judging |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applications per week (active search) | 15–40 tailored | < 10 | 2 weeks |
| Applications → any response | ≥ 5% (cold), ≥ 30% (referred) | < 3% cold | 40 applications |
| Response → first-round screen | ≥ 50% | < 30% (often failing OAs) | 6 responses |
| First-round → final/onsite | ≥ 30% | < 20% | 5 screens |
| Final/onsite → offer | ≥ 25% | < 15% | 4 finals |

#### Adjustments
- **Needs sponsorship**: expect cold response rates around half of the above. Before calling it a resume problem,
  check what share of applications went to companies that sponsor.
- **Referrals**: compute referred and cold separately when possible. A low referred rate points more to
  resume/fit than to visibility.
- **Off-cycle timing**: applying months after a new-grad posting opened depresses response rates. Ask about
  how many days after posting they usually apply.
- **Small samples**: below the minimum sample, say "too early to tell" for that stage and give the number needed.

#### Picking the stage
1. Not enough volume for the time spent (below the weekly floor, or < 40 total after 3+ weeks) → **Stage 0**.
2. Otherwise, the earliest conversion in the "concerning" band with enough sample → that stage.
3. If nothing is concerning but there's no offer yet, it's likely variance or timing. Name the weakest relative stage,
   say the evidence is mild, and set a re-check date.

Choose **one primary stage**, and at most one secondary:

| Stage | Signal | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 0 Not enough volume | Few applications for the time spent, or "still polishing" | Ship now, set a weekly cadence |
| 1 No interviews | Plenty of applications, response rate below benchmark | Direction, JD match, application records |
| 2 Interviews stall | Screens happen, few advance | Role understanding, motivation, answer structure, mock review |
| 3 Can't close / offer decision | Finals happen, no offer, or an offer to evaluate | Final-round closing, offer evaluation, team & manager |
| 4 Accepted | Offer signed | Ramp-up plan |

When two stages look weak, pick the **earliest** weak one with enough sample size. Upstream fixes change what flows
downstream. Five screens isn't enough to judge Stage 2. Say so rather than over-read it.

### 3. Run that stage's checks
Use [references/stage-playbooks.md](references/stage-playbooks.md). Only open the section for the chosen stage(s).
If a resume or JDs were given, make the checks concrete: quote the bullet, name the missing keyword, point to the
seniority mismatch. Apply US norms: no photo, US Letter, one page for new grads, no age/marital status.

### 4. Write the report
Follow [references/report-template.md](references/report-template.md): where you're stuck (with the numbers),
why (evidence), what to keep, 3–5 ordered tasks for the next two weeks with a "done" check for each, and what to
bring back for the re-check.

Keep it short and direct. Don't use fear language like "it might be too late". Don't list every possible job-search
tip. If the data truly can't support a diagnosis, say what single number would settle it and ask for it.
