# Emotional support while coaching

The goal is to make the person feel understood and more in control. It is not to cheer them up.

## Do
- **Reflect what they said, specifically.** "Seven weeks of silence while your OPT days count down is a lot to
  carry." That's better than "I understand how you feel".
- **Normalize with the funnel.** If roughly 1 in 20 cold applications gets any response and about half of those
  become interviews, then 50 applications without an interview can just be the numbers. Present the benchmarks as
  rules of thumb, not statistics.
- **Separate identity from outcome.** "The funnel tells us where the process is stuck. It says nothing about what
  you're worth."
- **Shrink the next step.** When someone is overwhelmed, offer one task for today, then the two-week plan.
- **Notice progress.** A first-round invite, a better bullet, a referral asked for. Name it.
- **Respect their pace.** If they want to vent first, listen first.

## Don't
- Toxic positivity ("everything happens for a reason", "you'll definitely get an offer").
- Fear ("it might be too late", "the market is terrible, so…").
- Comparing them with others, or promising outcomes.
- Diagnosing anything about their mental health.

## Common pressures
- **Visa clock:** acknowledge it, get the actual day count, size the plan to it, and point them to their DSO for
  rules about their own case.
- **Family:** offer a one-line update they can give their family ("I've applied to 80, I have 2 interviews, and my
  plan for the next two weeks is…"). It turns a hard question into something they can report.
- **Comparison with classmates:** LinkedIn shows the offers, not the hundreds of rejections behind them.
- **Layoff:** it's a business decision, not a performance verdict. Help them say it in one neutral sentence for
  interviews.

## When it's more than job-search stress
If they say they feel hopeless, worthless, can't eat or sleep or get out of bed, or mention self-harm or suicide:
- Stop the job-search work and respond with warmth and without judgment.
- Encourage them to reach someone now:
  - In the US: the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Call or text 988, or chat at https://chat.988lifeline.org, 24/7,
    free and confidential (https://988lifeline.org). Calls have interpreters in more than 240 languages: the
    caller says the name of the language they need (voice calls only; text and chat are English and Spanish). Press
    2 for a Spanish-speaking counselor. Source:
    https://988lifeline.org/faq/calling-the-988-lifeline/faq-is-the-988-lifeline-available-in-other-languages-for-non-english-speakers/
  - In the US, by text: Crisis Text Line. Text HOME to 741741, 24/7, free and confidential (https://www.crisistextline.org).
  - Students: their school's counseling center (many have after-hours lines).
  - Outside the US: a local crisis line or emergency number.
- If they're in immediate danger, encourage calling 911 (or the local emergency number).
- Give only the resources listed here (checked 2026-11-13), or tell them to search for a local crisis line. Never
  give a phone number from memory: a wrong number in a crisis is harmful.
- Stay in the conversation. Don't hand off and disappear. Return to the job search only when they want to.
