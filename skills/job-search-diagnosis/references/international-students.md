# International students (F-1, OPT, STEM OPT, H-1B)

Basics that change a job-search plan. These were checked against official pages on 2026-11-13. Rules change: for
anything about their own case, send them to their school's DSO (Designated School Official) or an immigration
attorney. Don't give legal advice.

## The unemployment clock
- Post-completion OPT allows at most **90 days of unemployment**. A 24-month STEM OPT extension adds 60 days, for
  **150 days in total**.
  Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub/stem-opt-extension-overview
- In coaching: ask how many days they have used, and size the plan to it. If they're close to the limit, it's
  worth asking their DSO what work counts toward staying employed. Don't guess the rules for them.

- After the program and any authorized practical training end, F-1 students have **60 days** to leave the US,
  transfer to another school, change education level, or apply to change status (e.g. to H-1B).
  Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/maintaining-status
  In coaching: when someone is near the end of OPT, this is useful to know, but what applies to their own dates is
  a question for their DSO.

## STEM OPT and E-Verify
- A STEM OPT extension must be with an employer enrolled in **E-Verify**.
  Source: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub/stem-opt-frequently-asked-questions
- In coaching: for STEM students, check whether target employers use E-Verify.

## H-1B
- Cap-subject H-1B petitions go through an annual electronic registration, typically in **March**. Petitions start
  on or after **October 1** of the fiscal year. For FY 2027, USCIS uses a **weighted selection by wage level** when a
  random selection is needed, instead of a plain lottery.
  Source: https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/h-1b-electronic-registration-process
- **Cap-exempt employers** are not subject to the annual cap: institutions of higher education, their affiliated or
  related nonprofits, nonprofit research organizations, and government research organizations.
  Source: https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations
- In coaching: if the cap is a worry, cap-exempt employers (universities, research institutes) can be a real target
  list, not a fallback.

## Applying
- **Answer the sponsorship question honestly.** A wrong answer causes worse problems later than a rejection now.
- **Target sponsors.** A low response rate from cold applications to companies that don't sponsor isn't a resume
  problem. Before rewriting anything, check what share of their applications went to companies that sponsor. Public
  sources: the Department of Labor's H-1B LCA disclosure data, and sites built on it.
- **Referrals matter more.** Alumni who were international students understand the situation and are often
  willing to help.

## The pressure behind it
Research backs treating this as real, separate stress:
- Acculturative stress correlates with depression at r = .41 in international students (Amlashi et al. 2024, meta).
- It lowers what students expect of their own careers (Reynolds & Constantine 2007).
- In interviews with F-1 students, visa uncertainty was its own stressor. Contingency plans, peer
  information-sharing and support helped (Domínguez et al. 2022).

So help them build a Plan B and C (cap-exempt employers, STEM OPT, other countries, going home for a while), and
connect them with peers and campus resources. Sources: [evidence.md](evidence.md).

International students often carry a visa deadline, tuition already spent, family expectations from far away, and
time-zone isolation. Acknowledge this specifically. Then turn it into something they can control: their day count,
their target list, this week's applications.
