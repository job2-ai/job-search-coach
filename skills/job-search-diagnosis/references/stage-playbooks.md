# Stage playbooks

Open only the chosen stage. Each section has checks (what to look at) and tasks (what to assign). Pick 3–5 tasks
that the checks actually support. Don't assign every task.

---

## Stage 0 — Not enough volume

Typical cause: waiting until the resume, LinkedIn, portfolio, and LeetCode are all "ready".

Checks
- Is there a usable resume now? If it's one page with a few real projects or internships, it's usable.
- What's blocking sending: perfectionism, unclear targets, or time (classes, thesis)?

Tasks
- Send applications this week with the current resume. Keep improving it in parallel.
- Set a weekly cadence that fits their schedule (e.g. 3 sessions × 1 hour, 5 applications each).
- Make a simple tracker (company, role, date posted, date applied, channel, status).
- Pick one material to improve per week. Don't rebuild everything at once.

## Stage 1 — Applying, no interviews

Look at three things in this order: **direction → JD match → application records.**

Checks — direction
- Are the target roles coherent? A resume aimed at 3+ unrelated role types usually fits none.
- Seniority: are they applying to roles asking for 2–5 years? New grads should filter for "new grad", "entry",
  "early career", "university", "I/Associate", or "0–2 years".
- Is the target realistic for their background, or does it need a bridge role first?

Checks — JD match (needs resume + JDs)
- Do the JD's top 5–8 required skills and keywords appear on the resume in the same words?
- Can a recruiter see the top third of the page in 10 seconds and tell what role this person is for?
- Do bullets show outcome or scope (numbers, users, latency, revenue, scale) or only duties?
- Format: one page, US Letter, plain single column parseable by an ATS, no photo, no personal details beyond contact info.

Checks — application records
- Timing: applying within ~1 week of posting, or weeks late?
- Channel: zero referrals or recruiter contact? Cold-only searches convert poorly.
- Sponsorship: share of applications to companies that don't sponsor (for those who need it).
- Location constraints that are too narrow.

Tasks (pick from)
- Narrow to 1–2 role families. Write a one-line target ("Backend SWE new grad, Python/Go, US-wide").
- Make one base resume per role family. Tailor the top section and skills to each JD (about 10 minutes, not a rewrite).
- Rewrite the 3 weakest bullets as action + what + measurable result.
- Ask for 5 referrals a week through alumni, classmates, ex-interns, and LinkedIn second-degree connections, with a short specific message.
- Set a "fresh postings" routine: check new-grad postings every 2–3 days and apply within the week.
- For sponsorship needs: filter targets to known sponsors before applying.

## Stage 2 — Getting interviews, not advancing

Look at four things: **role understanding, motivation, answer structure, and how they practice.**

Checks
- Where exactly do they drop: OA, recruiter screen, technical screen, or hiring-manager/behavioral?
- Role understanding: can they say in two sentences what this team does and what the role's first 6 months look like?
- Motivation: do they have a specific "why this company / why this role" that isn't generic?
- Answer structure: behavioral answers in STAR with their own actions and a result. Technical: do they think aloud,
  clarify, test?
- Practice: have they done any recorded or live mocks, and reviewed them? Repeating questions alone doesn't count.

Tasks (pick from)
- Write and rehearse a 60–90 second "tell me about yourself" for each role family.
- Build a story bank: 6–8 STAR stories mapped to common prompts (conflict, failure, ambiguity, leadership, impact).
- For each upcoming interview, a one-page prep sheet: team/product, role's likely problems, 2 tailored "why" points,
  3 questions to ask.
- Two mock interviews per week with feedback, plus a written review of each: what went wrong and the fix.
- Technical: practice the round type they fail in (OA timed sets, live coding with narration, case/SQL, design),
  not generic grinding.
- Write a debrief within 24 hours after every real interview. Use it to choose next week's practice.

## Stage 3 — Final rounds without offers, or evaluating an offer

Checks — not closing
- Pattern across finals: same round type failing? Weak "why us"? Questions at the end weak or missing?
- Follow-through: thank-you notes, responsiveness, and asking the recruiter for feedback.
- Are they being out-competed on a specific skill that the feedback mentions?

Checks — evaluating an offer
- Team and manager: what they'll actually work on, how the manager supports new grads, and team stability.
- Growth: mentorship, feedback cadence, promotion expectations for new grads.
- Total comp vs market (base, bonus, equity vesting, sign-on), location and cost of living.
- Visa: H-1B sponsorship policy and timeline, STEM OPT employer (E-Verify) eligibility.
- Deadlines and leverage: other processes in flight.

Tasks (pick from)
- Ask each final-round recruiter for specific feedback. Log it.
- Prepare a strong close: 3 thoughtful questions per interviewer and a 30-second "why I fit" recap.
- Ask the manager or a team member for a 20-minute chat before deciding.
- Write the offer comparison table. Negotiate once, politely, with data or a competing offer.
- Ask for a deadline extension if other finals are in progress.

## Stage 4 — Accepted, starting soon

Tasks
- Before day 1: learn the product, the team's stack and domain basics, and what success looks like at 90 days.
- 30/60/90 plan: learn → ship a small thing → own a slice. Agree on it with the manager in week 1.
- Weekly 1:1 notes: what I did, what's blocking me, what I need.
- Write down how things work as you learn them. Docs written by new hires are valued and get you visibility.
