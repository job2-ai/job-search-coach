# Drill-down questions

Use these when the numbers point at a stage but you need one more fact to be sure, or to pick the right tasks. Ask
one or two at a time, never the whole list.

## Telling stages apart
- "Of the companies that answered, how many sent an online assessment, and how many invited you to talk?"
  Many assessments and no talks point to failing assessments, which is Stage 2.
- "When you hear nothing, is it silence or a rejection email?" Fast rejections often come from an automatic filter
  (level, location, sponsorship question). Silence points more to visibility and timing.
- "Where do interviews end: recruiter call, technical round, or hiring manager?"
- "Did any final round give you feedback?"

## Stage 0: not enough volume
- "What's stopping you from sending this week?" (perfectionism, unclear target, no time, fear of rejection)
- "If the resume were good enough today, how many hours a week could you give to applying?"

## Stage 1: no interviews
- "What exact titles are you searching for? Paste two postings you applied to."
- "How many days after a posting goes up do you usually apply?"
- "How many came through a referral or someone you messaged?"
- International students: "How do you answer the sponsorship question, and do you check whether the company
  sponsors before applying?"

## Stage 2: interviews stall
- "Tell me the last question you felt went badly. What did you say?" Then coach that one answer.
- "In 30 seconds, why this company?" Listen for something specific to that company.
- "How have you practiced: alone, out loud, recorded, with someone?"

## Stage 3: closing or deciding
- "After the final, did you send thank-you notes and ask the recruiter for feedback?"
- "What do you know about the manager and the team you'd join?"
- "What's the deadline, and what else is still in progress?"

## Feelings, when they come up
- "How are you holding up with all this?" Ask once, early, and mean it.
- "What's the part that worries you most right now?" Often it's not the resume (a visa date, a parent's question,
  a classmate's offer). Address that directly, then return to what they can control.
